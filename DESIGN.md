# Design System: Waypoint

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "Real streets through game worlds"**

Waypoint makes real navigation feel like a game atlas. The map occupies the main surface; controls borrow the selected game's composition, lettering, material and marker grammar. The user's confirmed direction is: “Keep each game's distinct control layout, but polish it.” Consistency comes from touch sizing, legibility and behavior, rather than identical control positions.

This records the implemented Android system, not a claim of exact game reproduction. The app uses authored graphics, sourced game font families and identified readable adaptations with real geographic data. Reference screenshots remain research evidence. Search, settings, destination flows and personal Quests retain native Android conventions. Quests inherits the selected world’s palette, framing and heading character while its task text, fields and actions remain native and readable.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Distinct map compositions for 38 game-inspired worlds; no general-purpose Classic choice.
- Game-specific cartography, materials, pointers and place markers.
- An animated native welcome screen, a searchable world catalogue with previews and favourites, and visible control captions.
- Native touch targets and readable navigation data.
- Actual streets, GPS state and route progress; personal tasks and earned rewards live in the separate Quests destination.

Ground truth is `WelcomeScreen.kt`, `MainActivity.kt`, `GameHud.kt`, `GameAtlasHud.kt`, `FantasyAtlases.kt`, `ModernAtlases.kt`, `Cartography.kt`, `MapGlyphs.kt`, `WorldMap.kt`, `NavigationController.kt`, `WorldLibrary.kt`, `IssueSevenAtlases.kt`, `StalkerAtlas.kt`, `NierAtlas.kt`, `AdaptiveRoutePreview.kt`, `SavedPlaces.kt`, `UpdateAlertPermissions.kt`, `WorldTypography.kt`, `VehicleMarkers.kt`, `WaypointCarOverlay.kt`, `WorldVoice.kt`, `WorldVoiceLines.kt`, `QuestScreens.kt`, `QuestEditor.kt`, `QuestColors.kt`, `QuestCelebration.kt` and the generated style assets. Frontmatter contains reused representative tokens, not a second copy of every world palette. Default component colors represent the Los Santos/dark family; the explicitly named Machine Atlas and transport specimens show its light family. Components resolve the current world at runtime. Heights of native action tokens are minimum heights, not a cap on font scaling. The sidecar’s browser snippets are documentation translations: dp/sp are rendered as CSS pixels at baseline scale and browser focus outlines are preview affordances; Compose remains the implementation authority. Android dimensions retain `dp` and `sp`; car Canvas tokens use logical `px` before viewport scaling. Eight-digit colors use CSS `RRGGBBAA` ordering, converted from Kotlin `AARRGGBB`.

## Colors

Each atlas has a four-role HUD palette: panel, foreground, accent and muted text. Its cartography has separate land, park, water, building, road, casing, label and outline colors. HUD accent and route color are deliberately independent.

### Primary

- **Launcher route lime and arrow ivory** form the original two-facet navigation mark and bent route. These identity colors are scoped to the full-color launcher icon.
- **Default navigation green** marks shared navigation actions and selected states in the neutral dark family.
- **Western cream and scarlet** pair quiet paper controls with a red route.
- **Night cyan and yellow** distinguish technical labels from guidance over the red-building city map.
- **Terminal phosphor** carries the green monochrome interface without making every road as bright as its controls.

### Secondary

- **Slate gold** outlines the blue fantasy instrument surface.
- **Machine Atlas rust** marks selected HUD actions and opaque task surfaces. The warmer route rust has its own token and uses the shared dark route casing; the pale catalogue accent belongs on the dark library surface.
- **Exclusion Zone amber** marks selected controls against charcoal PDA panels, while a separate green route stays readable over grey/olive terrain. Its muted ink and grey-green border carry secondary labels and framing.
- **Dark Zone orange**, **DOS amber**, **tactical sand**, **vector blue**, **field sage** and **retro gold** give the new dark families distinct control accents.
- **Sea Chart russet**, **Aincrad bronze** and **console blue** provide readable action ink on the new light families. Their route colors remain separate cartographic roles.
- World-specific accents continue in `hudPalette(world)` and `World.routeColor`; do not replace them with the default green when extending a theme.

### Neutral

- **Launcher map ink and muted compass** support the arrow with a subdued abstract map and open brackets. Android supplies the colors for themed-icon rendering from the monochrome layer.
- **Library ink, cream and muted** support search, settings, saved places and the theme catalogue, including personal-name editing and automatic-update status.
- **Machine Atlas beige, charcoal and muted ink** keep its square HUD quiet and readable. Its opaque task surface is distinct from its translucent map panel. Separate map ground, land, road and building tones preserve real geographic masses; they are not a paper texture.
- **Default and western panels** give text a nearly opaque local backing over changing geography.
- **Daylight panel and ink** reverse the contrast for Nudle-style controls. Horizon also uses a light panel with dark text.

**The Route Separation Rule.** Road, route and selected destination must remain distinguishable in the selected world. Map color changes do not justify hiding street connectivity.

**The Paired Contrast Rule.** Choose foreground and panel as a pair from the world palette. For text directly over the map, use the explicit light or dark treatment of that atlas instead of inheriting a panel foreground blindly.

Quests and full world previews use `QuestTheme`: the selected HUD panel becomes opaque, its luminance selects the light or dark Material scheme, and text/action ink is paired with that surface. `questInk` retains an existing pair when it reaches 4.5:1; otherwise it moves that ink toward black or white in 5% steps until the threshold is reached. Outlines use 3:1. Primary/secondary containers composite the adjusted accent at 14% over the surface; surface variants composite the foreground at 8%. This is a task-surface adaptation, not a replacement for the world’s map or route palette. Status and navigation bar ink follows the task/preview surface and restores on exit.

**The Task Ink Rule.** Use the world’s opaque task surface and contrast-paired Material roles for Quests and world previews. Decorative map accents are not automatically suitable for body text or filled-button labels.

## Typography

Barlow Condensed is the default bold atlas face. Old West pairs Chinese Rocks display titles with Kirsty reading text; Los Santos, San Andreas and Liberty City use Pricedown Black titles. Yellowtail supplies the Vice map title and Cinzel selected fantasy atlas titles. Pixel World uses Minecraft Seven, while 16-bit Quest retains Monocraft. Dark Zone uses Borda, Tactical Radar uses Stratum2, Sea Chart uses Windlass titles, and Skyrim uses Futura Condensed Medium. Roboto Condensed supplies Green Terminal and Exclusion Zone lettering; the latter is a readable PDA adaptation. Libre Baskerville supplies serif HUD labels, Rajdhani technical labels and Noto Sans daylight/contemporary controls and both Machine Atlas title and reading roles. Machine Atlas uses Noto Sans as an identified readable adaptation, not the original NieR:Automata font. Normal search fields, destination details and settings use Material typography for native text behavior.

The frontmatter records observed representative roles. `AtlasText` normally uses one line with ellipsis and a line height four scaled pixels above the selected text size. Important destination names permit two lines. In the Worlds catalogue, the map-style name uses `worldFont(world)` with Material `titleLarge` at the `catalogue-title` scale. The inspiration name beneath uses native `bodySmall` at the `catalogue-inspiration` scale. Catalogue titles are not forced into one line. The full preview uses a world-font headline and native descriptive text. These catalogue roles are independent of onboarding’s local type choices.

Unavailable ranges fall back to provider Noto Sans coverage. The internal stack name `Waypoint Classic` means neutral Noto Sans lettering; it is not a selectable world. `docs/map-fonts.md` records the font roles, licenses and substitution limits.

**The Lettering Identity Rule.** Preserve the selected world's display character while keeping native form text and route details readable. Do not turn every game into the same terminal or pixel font.

**The Catalogue Hierarchy Rule.** World catalogue cards lead with the map-style name in the world display face, then a smaller native inspiration name and a labelled Preview world action. Keep that hierarchy stable across the catalogue while allowing text to wrap.

Quests uses the selected `worldFont` for `headlineMedium` and `hudFont` for `titleLarge`; native Material body, label, field and button styles are retained. The title and HUD reading roles follow the shared mapping above, including Borda for Dark Zone, Stratum2 for Tactical Radar, Windlass/Libre Baskerville for Sea Chart and Roboto Condensed for Exclusion Zone. The focus countdown uses Share Tech Mono on the inherited Material display role. Native type sizes and line heights continue to scale with Android settings; the frontmatter does not freeze unspecified Material body sizes.

## Layout

A native welcome screen precedes the idle map. Its scrollable content includes a short headline and selected-world preview, while a bottom action opens the map. Worlds and Settings remain reachable before map rendering begins. An ordinary fresh launch shows the welcome screen even if a selected place remains in memory. Active guidance and an external destination launch bypass it; merely having a selected place does not.

The normal phone atlas is a full map with edge-anchored Compose layers. Each world owns its arrangement. Shared primitives supply behavior, not a universal header and footer.

Atlas map actions include visible captions. The adaptive route toolbar uses native Back and Settings icon buttons with semantic labels and a separate Route title. `AtlasTool` is a 64dp-wide clickable column with a minimum 70dp height and a 4dp outer gutter; its icon plate is 48dp square with 21dp artwork. Its caption uses Roboto Condensed at the `control-caption` scale, with zero tracking, one line, no soft wrapping and ellipsis on overflow. It sits on its own theme-backed surface. The column merges its semantics into one button. Action rows and search launchers have a minimum 48dp height plus their 4dp outer gutter. Pixel hotbar slots are 64dp wide with a 56dp sprite plate and the same Roboto Condensed caption treatment; the artwork remains pixel-rendered. The location icon caption is Locate. These are code dimensions, not an approval of any clipping encountered in QA.

Below 420dp of available atlas height, `CompactAtlas` provides the implemented compact arrangement. At 600dp available width and above, the main screen replaces its bottom navigation with a rail. Map, Worlds, Saved and Quests are the four labelled destinations while the idle app is visible; active guidance and onboarding suppress this main navigation. Below 500dp available window height, the rail drops its decorative icon and spacer. Active-guidance layouts retain the compact left/right arrangement and a lower panel that can scroll up to 300dp high.

A selected destination uses `AdaptiveRoutePreview` when the available window height is below 500dp or Android font scale exceeds 1.3. A stable, minimum-56dp toolbar sits above one bounded vertical scroll area containing the four transport choices, map error if present, GPS status, Use my location, the destination card and attribution. The outer padding is 12dp, with 10dp between toolbar and details and 12dp between detail sections. At a width of at least 600dp with font scale at most 1.3, this composition occupies the right 58% of the map viewport; other adaptive cases use the available width. The normal portrait composition retains its separate upper and lower overlays. These are source rules; corrected 200% text and compact-landscape captures remain pending review.

The Quests list and catalogue center their content within a maximum 760dp width. Quest editor/detail content uses a maximum 720dp; primary edit/preview actions use 680dp, and the list’s Add a quest action uses 720dp. Task pages use 20dp or 24dp horizontal insets with 8dp, 12dp, 16dp and 20dp gaps. Below 500dp screen height, Quests omits the introductory line and progress block, keeping tabs, task rows and the fixed Add area available. Its Add button has a minimum 52dp height in a separate bottom surface with 20dp horizontal and 12dp vertical padding. The list respects the Scaffold bottom inset so the action never substitutes for list padding. Active/Completed uses native scrollable tabs so large labels retain their width. Editor actions use IME insets to remain above the keyboard. Native interactive targets remain at least 48dp.

Projected car HUDs use the host-provided visible rectangle. The compact world readout anchors near its lower-right edge and shows the selected world title, with measured speed during guidance and a dash when GPS is unavailable. It has no extra eyebrow. Its navigating and idle dimensions are the two `car-world-readout` token variants, measured in logical pixels before a viewport-derived scale of 0.9–2.0. The title starts at the `car-world-title` size and can shrink to 15 logical pixels to fit; the speed uses `car-world-speed`. The readout is omitted below a 500×340px visible rectangle or when it cannot clear the guidance region. Host controls retain their own panes; this is an implementation description, not real-car validation.

These are implemented native adaptations. Earlier validation records include a Samsung Android 17 phone and tablet-sized display overrides on that phone. The Android 2.0 review packet establishes the normal Machine Atlas composition, saved-name dialog at the supplied normal/200% sizes, prominent Quests, update explanations and the recorded Worlds restoration checkpoint. Its large-text route findings required the current adaptive changes; the corrected 200% route, compact-landscape and final preview captures have not yet received a visual verdict. This update does not establish motion smoothness, physical-tablet or projected-car validation. Saved-name editing with the keyboard open, TalkBack traversal, hardware keyboard use, older Android devices and the five-person novice usability study remain unverified.

Route-preview camera fitting uses measured bounds from `onGloballyPositioned` in root pixels. Normal portrait uses the map, header and destination-panel bounds to leave a clear middle with 28dp of breathing room and system-inset handling. The adaptive side panel supplies a measured right inset; full-width adaptive details do not reuse the old independent-overlay inset calculation. The fitted bounds include route geometry, actual GPS origin and the selected destination. Route overview explicitly sets bearing and tilt to zero, keeping it north-up and flat rather than inheriting the world's idle camera perspective. Fitting responds to measured-inset, layout and style changes. This describes the implementation, not a visibility guarantee across untested layouts.

Insets reserve the status and gesture areas. A compact, labelled Map info button at the lower edge opens provider attribution; a persistent wide attribution footer is no longer part of the composition. Selected-place and active-guidance states prioritize the next maneuver and destination actions; they share a navigation arrangement colored by the current theme. Worlds is available in idle, preview and active guidance. Choosing a world changes the appearance without clearing the selected destination or route.

**The Distinct Layout Rule.** Keep the game-specific idle composition. Improve spacing and reachability inside that composition before moving controls into a common shell.

**The Visible Action Rule.** Atlas icon actions need short visible captions as well as semantic descriptions. Use Search, Locate, Worlds, Settings, Map tilt, Mute or Sound on rather than requiring users to infer the icon alone.

## Elevation & Depth

HUD panels primarily use flat color, opacity, borders and local contrast. They do not rely on large ambient shadows. Quest pages and list rows retain flat native surfaces; the transient reward surface uses Material tonal elevation of 6dp and shadow elevation of 4dp so it sits above the current page. These local reward elevations do not become a general card shadow. Depth belongs to real geographic relief and selected building extrusions. Night City, Pixel World, Manhattan and Machine Atlas use different map camera and building treatments; parchment atlases remain flat. Machine Atlas starts with a 42-degree tilt, real building extrusions and restrained neutral map masses; its score-rule chrome is static.

The paper fibre and pixel grass, stone and water textures are authored raster assets. Pixel controls use discrete sprites and nearest-neighbor rendering. Canvas ornament supplies geometric corners, bands and compass details; it is not a replacement for a hand-painted fictional map.

The welcome content enters with a 360ms fade and 420ms upward slide, travelling only 3.5% of its height. Changing the selected welcome world uses a 240ms crossfade. The heading rose turns through the shortest arc over 120ms. Vehicle position uses a bounded 500–1500ms interpolation matched to accepted GPS cadence (1000ms after a long interruption), never extrapolating beyond a fix. Repeated sensor readings do not restart that timeline. The following camera and vehicle share the rendered position and driving heading; a separate heading-only change uses 180ms. Car zoom, padding and follow recovery ease over 450ms under the same frame owner. Phone follow recovery uses 500ms and explicit recentering uses 700ms, with a bounded handoff that prevents competing camera animations. Main destinations use Material fade-through (180ms entry after a 40ms delay, 100ms exit); quest rows use 220ms placement and 150/100ms entry/exit. Android Remove animations disables spatial interpolation and native Compose transitions honor the system duration scale. Motion belongs to state changes rather than an always-running decorative loop. It must not imply a heading is known when sensor confidence is insufficient.

Scrolling uses native scroll containers and stable keyed world rows. Catalogue scroll state is owned outside the preview branch and saved with the Worlds destination, so preview, apply, Back and activity recreation retain the offset, query and filter. `withReadyWindowInsets` defers the restored content’s first measurement until Compose and root system-bar insets agree; it uses no timed scroll correction. The review packet confirms the recorded anchor checkpoint, not approval of the temporary artwork visible in those captures. Both thumbnail and full preview-image decoding run off the UI thread. Frame-by-frame map bookkeeping does not invalidate Compose state. Phone frame callbacks stop when the map is hidden or the lifecycle is not resumed; car callbacks cancel before style replacement and destruction. `MapSwatch` caches and batches thumbnail paths through `drawWithCache`. Its `onDrawBehind` drawing is enclosed by `clipRect`, containing the ground, buildings, streets and route within the preview bounds rather than drawing into the description row. The welcome screen does not create the native map. Once created, a hidden MapView is paused/stopped behind the picker, search and modal surfaces, while preserving navigation state; map updates resume when visible. Heading samples continue into the arbiter while lists or welcome content cover the map, but heading-driven UI emissions are gated unless the map, sensor settings or car display needs them. Returning to a visible heading surface publishes the current reading. When reliability, source and status are unchanged, bearing changes smaller than 0.3 degrees do not trigger a new UI state. These are implemented performance measures, not a measured frame-rate claim.

Circular location controls remain part of the western family; pixel slots retain their bevelled, rectilinear treatment. ### Personal quests

The task journal uses native list, detail and editor screens. Task titles and actions lead; world lettering, portraits and restrained framing carry the selected theme. Empty Active lists offer Everyday task, Visit a place and Focus session templates; earned progress appears only after activity and can be hidden. Use explicit Complete quest and Undo/reopen actions. **The Explicit Completion Rule.** Keep task completion under the user’s control with an immediate Undo path. Timer and navigation state inform the task without silently completing it.

**The Native Task Rule.** Keep task body text, fields, actions, scrolling and system navigation native. Apply world identity through paired colors, headings, shapes and original portraits or completion cues.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** preserve the selected world's layout, material and lettering while improving contrast and spacing.
- **Do** keep real roads, route guidance and the position marker legible together.
- **Do** retain native touch targets, semantic labels, insets and system navigation behavior.
- **Do** consult the actual map or navigation state in `docs/visual-sourcebook.md` before changing a theme.
- **Do** show only reliable phone heading or travel course in the compass, with a dash when unavailable.
- **Do** keep the catalogue naming hierarchy consistent and visible captions on map icon controls.
- **Do** reserve fixed task actions outside the scrolling content and allow native text to reflow.
- **Do** use contrast-paired task colors, explicit completion with Undo, and quiet reward presentation outside navigation.
- **Do** preserve personal quests and progress when applying a different world.
- **Do** retain native scrolling, cached preview paths and paused hidden map rendering.

### Don't:

- **Don't** reduce all themes to one shared layout with changed colors.
- **Don't** reintroduce a non-game world or cancel navigation when changing a world.
- **Don't** add invented health, money or mission data to navigation. Personal quests belong in their own destination.
- **Don't** complete a quest merely because its timer ends or the user reaches a place.
- **Don't** gate worlds or navigation behind personal rewards or show reward flows while driving.
- **Don't** distort geography or hide roads to imitate fictional terrain.
- **Don't** treat posters, cinematics or decorative treasure maps as interchangeable with navigable map references.
- **Don't** describe substitute fonts, authored materials or mobile adaptations as exact proprietary game assets.

This file records current reusable rules. It does not canonize unresolved visual fidelity, clipped text, contrast defects, stale captures or unverified device behavior; those belong in review and validation records, including `docs/validation.md`.
